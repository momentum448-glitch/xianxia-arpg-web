# Environmental Storytelling

## 1. Purpose

Environmental storytelling uses space, objects, wear, arrangement and systems to let the player infer what a place is for, who uses it, what happened there and how it belongs to the larger world.

This is not the same as decorating a scene with lore props. The strongest environmental storytelling connects **function + evidence + composition**.

The GDC talk `What Happened Here? Environmental Storytelling` emphasizes environments as narrative devices that invite players to interpret implied events rather than receiving all information through exposition.

## 2. Start with activity, not prop names

Weak prompt:

> Add jars, baskets and herbs to the healer area.

Stronger design statement:

> This healer dries, sorts, stores and prepares medicinal plants for villagers and cultivators.

The second statement naturally generates useful evidence:

- drying rack;
- sorted bundles;
- storage jars;
- work surface;
- discarded stems;
- water vessel;
- cleaner open working space;
- greener planted edge.

Props become evidence of activity rather than a shopping list.

## 3. The five-question method

For every functional zone, answer:

1. **Who uses this place?**
2. **What do they repeatedly do here?**
3. **What tools/materials does that activity require?**
4. **What physical traces would repeated use leave?**
5. **What is unusual or story-specific about this place?**

This produces much more coherent scenes than generic “medieval village clutter.”

## 4. Evidence layers

### Functional evidence

Shows what the place does now.

Examples: goods, cart, herb rack, cooking setup, tools.

### Habit evidence

Shows repeated daily use.

Examples: worn ground, stacked containers, swept forecourt, repaired fence, water path, wheel ruts.

### Historical evidence

Shows age or past events.

Examples: old foundation, broken shrine, patched roof, replaced fence section, weathered marker.

### Social evidence

Shows status and relationships.

Examples: elder courtyard with more formal space; merchant area positioned near traffic; modest residential plots clustered around shared resources.

### World-system evidence

Shows xianxia-specific reality.

Examples: spirit-herb drying precautions, talisman marks, ward stones, faint cultivation residue, protective village seal, beast-damage repairs.

Use sparingly. If every basket glows with qi, the supernatural loses meaning.

## 5. Causal placement

A prop should have a reason to be where it is.

Examples:

- cart near the side/rear of the stall where loading is plausible;
- goods near the stall, not floating in the middle of a road;
- herbs near sunlight/airflow or a work area;
- water jars near a residence or preparation area;
- woodpile against a building edge;
- shrine/stone marker at a threshold or culturally meaningful node.

Causal placement creates believability even with a small asset kit.

## 6. Environmental storytelling through absence

Not every story cue is an added object.

Absence can communicate:

- abandoned yard;
- cleared ceremonial space;
- respected elder zone with little clutter;
- dangerous road where domestic props disappear;
- recently damaged area with missing fence segment.

Negative space can itself be narrative evidence.

## 7. Before-and-after logic

A useful test is to imagine the zone one hour before the player arrived.

Ask:

- What was the NPC doing?
- What object were they using?
- What route would a villager take through the space?
- What would need to stay clear?
- Where would supplies accumulate?
- What would be moved at night or in rain?

This helps prevent “museum display” composition where props exist only to face the camera.

## 8. Story density

Do not make every object tell a separate story. Strong scenes usually have:

- one dominant functional story;
- one or two supporting details;
- perhaps one optional historical/worldbuilding clue.

Too many narrative props create noise and weaken interpretation.

For mobile, tiny clues must also be readable at actual scale. If a story detail only works when zoomed into the source PNG, it is not serving the runtime.

## 9. Zone identity through activity

For settlement design, functional identity can be established without custom architecture for every zone.

Examples:

### Merchant

Core activity: receiving, displaying, storing and moving goods.

Evidence: stall, goods pile, sign, cart, traffic-adjacent forecourt.

### Healer

Core activity: collecting, drying, preparing and storing medicine.

Evidence: herbs, racks, jars, table, planted/green edge, tidy working clearance.

### Elder

Core activity: receiving villagers, teaching, discussing village/cultivation matters.

Evidence: calm courtyard, old tree or stone, seating/table element, lower clutter, symbolic marker.

### Residential

Core activity: cooking, storage, repair, water, fuel, domestic movement.

Evidence: wood, jars, simple fence, baskets, small gardens, laundry or tools if readable.

## 10. Xianxia specificity

The world should feel xianxia through selective rules, not by attaching ornate fantasy symbols to everything.

Possible layers:

- ordinary villagers coexist with cultivation culture;
- spiritually significant objects are rarer and more visually controlled;
- herbs, talismans, seals, sect influence and beast threats leave practical traces;
- architecture remains grounded in local material culture while supernatural elements create hierarchy.

This keeps the starting village believable and gives stronger contrast when the player reaches more mystical biomes.

## 11. Narrative environment pass

After the blockout is stable:

1. write one sentence describing the zone's activity;
2. identify the minimum evidence cluster;
3. assign one focal element;
4. assign supporting evidence;
5. place evidence where activity logically happens;
6. add one historical/world cue only if it strengthens identity;
7. remove props that do not support function, composition or story;
8. Phone QC without relying on labels.

## 12. Pass gates

A functional zone passes environmental-storytelling review when:

- a viewer can infer its broad function without the NPC title;
- props appear to be used, not displayed;
- placement has a plausible causal reason;
- the zone is visually distinct from neighboring functions;
- story detail does not obstruct movement/readability;
- xianxia-specific cues are present only where meaningful;
- the scene still works if some filler props are removed.
